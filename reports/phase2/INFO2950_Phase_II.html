Food and body-image themes in annotated social media posts¶

Github repo: AnyaChoudhary/info2950-fall2026-project

Key terms¶

  • ED means eating disorder. Pro-ED describes content that presents disordered eating positively or as a goal.
  • An annotation or code is a label assigned to content. It describes the post, not a diagnosis of its creator.
  • Eligible posts are the posts a coding question applies to. Twitter restraint uses the 326 eating-concern posts as its denominator.
  • A structural blank means the codebook skipped that question. It is not treated as a recorded zero.
  • EDE means Eating Disorder Examination. EDTT uses its four category names: Restraint, Eating Concern, Shape Concern, and Weight Concern. These are content labels here, not assessments of the people posting.
  • Shape concern refers to body shape or appearance. Eating concern refers to food and eating. Weight concern refers to body weight. These labels can overlap. A table's shape instead means rows and columns.
  • Restraint refers to deliberately limiting food intake.
  • Audio valence estimates musical positivity or brightness. Audio arousal estimates musical energy. Both range from 0 to 1 and do not measure a person's emotions.
  • Harmonic ratio describes the proportion of harmonic sound in the audio. Tempo is measured in beats per minute.
  • Format means video or photos (a photo slideshow) in EDTT.

1. Research questions¶

Our main question is: What food and body-image concerns appear in these posts, and how do they relate to other features of the posts?

  1. Which concerns and content features appear in each dataset, and how common are they among posts where each label applies?
  2. Among Twitter posts about eating disorders or body image, are shape-concern and eating-concern labels associated?
  3. In EDTT, is a restraint label associated with audio valence after accounting for whether the post is a video or photo slideshow?

The exploratory analysis addressed question 1. Questions 2 and 3 are planned for the final report and have not been tested. The files describe posts, so they cannot show algorithm effects, engagement effects, diagnoses, or changes in a person's relationship with food. Recovery and social comparison are not measured consistently enough to use as shared outcomes.

Twitter provides a larger sample of coded posts; EDTT provides a smaller sample with visual and audio features. We analyzed them separately because their collection methods and labels differ.

2.3 TikTok cleaning¶

The public EDTT data do not include post IDs. We assigned record_id from the original row order solely to track this file. We checked exact duplicates but did not infer duplicate posts from similar summaries or shared audio.

We standardized column names, trimmed surrounding whitespace, preserved annotation wording, checked numeric ranges and post format, and derived indicators from explicit category headers. Header matching is case-insensitive and allows a header after either a semicolon or a colon. This accommodates an observed separator inconsistency in the visual annotations. We left item-level spelling unchanged and did not reinterpret the annotation text.

A blank EDE subscale field remains unknown for all four derived EDE indicators. It is not recoded to four zeros. A zero on a nonblank field means the category was not recorded there, not that a clinical symptom is absent. We also preserved blank diagnostic fields because the dictionary did not explain their meaning. We checked the audio labels against the dictionary's thresholds and reported disagreements without changing the original values.

def header_flag(series, header):

    return series.str.contains(pattern, case=False, regex=True).astype('Int64')

2.4 What we kept¶

The cleaning produced two separate analysis tables. We kept the datasets separate because their posts cannot be matched and their collection methods differ. Eleven Twitter rows have noconcern=0 while all three concern fields are zero. We flagged these rows in noconcern_inconsistent, retained the original codes, and saved their synthetic IDs separately. The aversion denominator continues to follow the original source eligibility code, not a reconstructed universe. Without the original posts, we cannot determine which label is wrong. No rows were removed from EDTT; Twitter excluded only the 342 unrelated and 74 unknown-relevance rows from its main analysis. We did not fill missing values, remove outliers, remove repeated annotation patterns, or change structural blanks to zero.

Intermediate files preserve the complete validated Twitter release and normalized EDTT release. Processed files preserve the variables needed for future analysis. The table below records the number of posts retained. Any later changes to these exclusions will be documented.

3. Data description¶

What a row means¶

Each row is one post, not one person or account. Percentages and planned models describe posts in the samples. Multiple posts could come from the same person, but the released files do not identify those groups.

Rows and columns¶

Table Rows Columns What is included
Twitter raw 3,000 17 Row ID and 16 annotation codes
Twitter cleaned 2,584 18 All 17 source columns plus a conflict flag, only related posts
EDTT raw 85 13 Format, five text fields, five numeric audio fields, and two audio labels
EDTT cleaned 85 26 All 13 source fields, row number, four EDE labels, seven visual labels, and an EDE category count

Twitter has more posts but fewer kinds of measures. Its ID is an identifier. Its relevance code allows 0, 1, or 9. The remaining codes are 0 or 1 where applicable. EDTT has fewer posts and more text and audio features. Only 79 posts have nonblank EDE text, which limits the precision of the planned model.

Why the datasets were made¶

Twitter: One row represents one sampled post. The analysis table has 2,584 rows and retains the 17 source columns plus a logical-inconsistency flag. id is a synthetic row identifier. The remaining fields are categorical annotations, not symptom severity scores. The release was created to characterize ED/body-image expression. Keyword selection and conditional coding determine which posts and features are represented. Multiple posts may come from one person, but this release cannot identify clusters.

EDTT: One row represents one curated TikTok post (video or photo slideshow). The release was created to study multimodal pro-ED content. The processed table has 85 rows. It keeps all 13 source fields and adds a row number, 11 category labels, and a count of recorded EDE categories. Text fields contain summaries and annotations, not the original private transcript. Audio valence and arousal are heuristic musical features, not the emotions of a creator or viewer. The original five numeric fields are tempo in beats per minute, valence, arousal, harmonic ratio, and major/minor mode.

No common clinical scale: Twitter's restraint field is assessed only inside eating-concern posts, whereas EDTT lists restraint as a separate category. Even shared labels do not establish identical measurement. We retained each source's definitions.

Privacy and consent¶

The Twitter paper reports public posts and an institutional determination that its study did not involve human subjects. That does not show that every poster knew about this specific secondary analysis or consented to it. The EDTT release describes privacy protections and withheld private fields. The inspected documentation does not establish individual notification or consent. We used aggregate summaries, avoided quoting sensitive post summaries, and made no attempts to identify creators. Post annotations cannot diagnose the people who posted them.

What the data can show¶

These tables support descriptive secondary analysis and prespecified associations between annotations within each sample. They are unsuitable for diagnosing creators, evaluating moderation decisions about particular accounts, estimating engagement, or ranking platforms by harm. There is no predefined train/test split. No prediction benchmark or model training is performed in Phase II. The analysis-ready files preserve source annotations so a reviewer can trace every derived flag back to its input. Questions about the original annotation or collection should be directed to the source authors rather than inferred from the summaries.

Cleaning and missing values¶

Twitter column names were lowercased and codes converted to nullable integers. We checked allowed values and skip rules, kept 2,584 related posts, and flagged 11 conflicts without changing them. There are no unexpected blanks under the codebook rules. For example, restraint is blank for 2,674 of the original 3,000 rows because it is coded only for the 326 posts with a recorded eating concern. The other rows include unrelated and unknown-relevance posts, not just posts coded as having no eating concern.

For EDTT, we standardized names, trimmed spaces, checked ranges, and extracted four EDE and seven visual category labels. The audio labels match the published thresholds, with zero disagreements. Six blank EDE subscale fields remain unknown. Another 58 rows have blank EDE diagnostic text. Its meaning is not explained, so diagnostic text is retained but not analyzed.

Neither dataset was merged, imputed, or filtered for outliers. Similar annotation patterns were retained. The cleaning steps and saved intermediate files are in Sections 2.2 through 2.4.

4. Limitations¶

  • Selected samples: Both datasets focused on eating-disorder-related content. Their frequencies do not represent all posts or social media users.
  • Different sources: Collection methods, formats, dates, and coding rules differed. We could not interpret differences between the samples as platform effects or changes over time.
  • Missing outcomes: Neither dataset included engagement, viewer exposure, or changes in food attitudes. These outcomes could not be studied with the available data.
  • Imperfect labels: Annotations reflect how researchers interpreted each post. The 11 conflicting Twitter records could not be resolved without the original content.
  • Missing annotations: Twitter blanks followed the coding rules, while six EDTT category fields had an unclear meaning. Treating these blanks as zero would change the denominators and misrepresent the evidence.
  • Small sample: Only 79 EDTT posts had the annotations needed for the planned model. Estimates from this group may be imprecise.
  • Repeated creators: Posts may share creators or audio. Without reliable identifiers, we could not account for this dependence, which may make confidence intervals too narrow.
  • Posts are not diagnoses: Content labels and musical scores do not establish a person's symptoms or feelings. Treating them as diagnoses could stigmatize creators.
  • Privacy: We reported aggregate results and avoided reproducing sensitive post summaries. Public availability did not establish that creators expected this use of their posts.
  • Removed content: Posts removed before collection may be absent. The files do not include removal histories, so the extent of this bias remains unknown.
  • Language and time: Twitter included English-language posts from January 2015. Changes in language and platform practices limit how well the sample describes current content.
  • Different definitions: The research teams used separate codebooks. Similar category names do not guarantee equivalent measurements across the datasets.

We therefore focused on patterns within each sample. The planned models will describe associations between post features, not effects on people's health or attitudes.

5.1 Twitter concerns¶

We first examined how often each concern appears. Since a post can have several labels, the percentages need not add to 100. We counted shape, eating, and weight separately. Their co-occurrence is reserved for the planned Twitter analysis.

Count the labels¶

For each label, we divided the number of recorded cases by the number of posts where that label applies. We excluded blank entries from each denominator without treating them as zeros.

In [16]:
# We counted posts with each label, using only posts where it applies.
def count_labels(posts, labels):
    results = []
    for column, label in labels.items():
        posts_counted = posts[column].notna().sum()
        posts_with_label = (posts[column] == 1).sum()
        percent = 100 * posts_with_label / posts_counted
        results.append([
            label, posts_with_label, posts_counted, percent
        ])

    return pd.DataFrame(results, columns=[
        'Label', 'Count', 'Eligible posts', "Percent"
    ])

5.5 What the EDA tells us¶

We retained the three research questions and the same rows. The figures answer different parts of question 1: label frequency, uncertainty from blank annotations, visual features by format, and audio distributions.

Shape concern is the main Twitter theme, while eating concern has a smaller but usable group. EDTT adds visual and audio measures, but only 79 posts have known EDE categories. These findings support one focused analysis per source rather than many comparisons.

Part 2. Preregistration of future analyses¶

What has been viewed¶

These plans follow the data audit and EDA, not an untouched dataset. We inspected source documentation, examples, the Twitter consistency check, individual-variable summaries, and visual features by format. We have not tabulated, correlated, or modeled the two planned predictor-outcome pairs.

These plans were clarified on October 19 before either model was fitted. We will retain this dated version and document any later changes.

Reporting plan¶

We will report both analyses whether or not their results support the hypotheses. Each report will include the posts used, model coefficients, predicted values, and limitations. We will compare the direction and size of each relationship rather than treating a coefficient alone as proof of a hypothesis.

On October 20, before fitting either model, we simplified these plans to focus on regression coefficients and predictions. The hypotheses and variables remained unchanged. Formal significance tests are not part of this version of the plan. Any later addition will be documented before it is run.

Both models will use individual posts as observations. Sample size affects precision, but it does not remove selection bias, confounding, or dependence between posts.

7. Preregistration 1: Twitter concerns¶

Hypothesis¶

Among related Twitter posts, posts with a shape-concern label are more likely to have an eating-concern label. Body-image and food concerns may appear together, but this is a hypothesis about content rather than illness or causation.

Data and variables¶

We will use the 2,584 related Twitter posts. The outcome will be eating (0 or 1), and the predictor will be shape (0 or 1). We will retain the original labels and exclude any row missing either variable. The cleaning checks found no such missing values.

Planned analysis¶

We will first count posts with and without eating concern in each shape-concern group and calculate the corresponding percentages. We will then fit a logistic regression using LogisticRegression:

logit[P(eating = 1)] = beta_0 + beta_1 * shape

We will report the intercept and shape coefficient, then calculate the predicted probability of eating concern for shape = 0 and shape = 1. A positive coefficient and a higher predicted probability for the shape-concern group would agree with the hypothesis. A zero or negative difference would not support its expected direction.

We will use the model's default regularization settings and record the library version and settings in the final report. The fitted probabilities may therefore differ slightly from the observed group percentages. We will show both, without treating the model's predictions as a significance test.

Checks and reporting¶

We will include both group sizes and all four combinations of the labels, so small groups are visible. The main analysis will keep the 11 flagged records. We will repeat the same comparison without those records and report whether the result changes. If a variable has no variation or fitting fails, we will report that limitation instead of changing the hypothesis.

Limits¶

The relationship may reflect coding practices or repeated creators. Account type and post length were unavailable. We will not use noconcern as a control because it is defined from the concern labels themselves. The results will describe this sample and will not establish a causal effect or a relationship across all Twitter posts.

8. Preregistration 2: Restraint and audio¶

Hypothesis¶

EDTT posts with a recorded restraint label have lower average audio valence than posts without that label, after accounting for format. This asks whether food-restriction content has a different musical tone. Upbeat or ironic music could produce the opposite result.

Data and variables¶

We will use the 79 posts with nonblank EDE text and valid audio valence and format. The outcome will be audio_valence on its 0-to-1 scale. The predictors will be ede_restraint and a binary format variable, coded as 1 for photos and 0 for videos. The six posts with unknown EDE categories will remain excluded from the primary analysis.

Planned analysis¶

We will fit a multiple linear regression using LinearRegression:

audio_valence = beta_0 + beta_1 * ede_restraint + beta_2 * photos + error

We will report the intercept and both coefficients. Within each format, we will compare predicted valence for restraint = 0 and restraint = 1. A negative restraint coefficient would agree with the hypothesis because it represents lower predicted valence at the same format. A zero or positive coefficient would not support its expected direction.

We will also report the observed group sizes and mean valence values. These will provide context for the model's predictions, especially if some combinations of restraint and format contain few posts.

Checks and reporting¶

We will compare observed and predicted values, inspect the residuals, and calculate mean squared error to summarize how closely the model fits these posts. This will describe fit to the sample, not performance on new posts. We will report any predictions outside 0 to 1 and keep all eligible rows rather than removing posts to improve the result.

If either predictor has no variation or the two predictors cannot be distinguished, we will report that the planned comparison could not be estimated reliably. As a separate missing-data check, we will repeat the model with all six unknown restraint labels set to 0, then with all six set to 1. These will be hypothetical scenarios, not corrections to the original labels or guaranteed bounds on the effect.

Limits¶

Format will account for one observed difference between posts, but it will not control for every possible confounder. Account type, post length, and reliable creator identifiers were unavailable. The small sample and possible reuse of audio will limit interpretation. Musical valence will not be treated as a measure of viewers' emotions or health.
